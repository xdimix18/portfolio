03, How I work

Four rules that do not change

This is what clients and employers notice most about working with me, and where I differ from the usual student portfolio.

Agreement before tools

First we clarify what it has to do and what it costs. A fixed quote before work starts, no hidden items and no changing the deal halfway through.

I keep going until it works

I hand over a result when it has been tested under real conditions, not when it looks like it works. With hardware and electronics that difference matters most.

Documentation with every job

Every job comes with a short note on what was done, which parts or names were used, and what to do if something acts up a week or a month later.

No dependence on someone else's cloud

Where possible, the solution stays on your equipment, your accounts and your data. That way you do not depend on someone else's subscription or decisions.

05, About

I started with hardware: microcontrollers, sensors and a soldering iron. My first jobs were in a repair shop, where I learned to take apart, measure, fix and return to service everything from phones and computers to power tools. Today that adds up to more than five years of working with equipment, including on site visits to clients.

Design came along the way. I have worked with 3D modelling and printing, and today I do 3D design and modelling in Fusion 360 and AutoCAD, with production handled by partner workshops. I also built my own studio monitor cabinets, and I do visuals and print preparation in Photoshop, Illustrator and InDesign. I am currently studying at the Faculty of Electrical Engineering in Podgorica, Applied Computing programme.

Alongside that I run my own server and a small homelab, where I set up AI tooling, automation, backups and services that I later offer to clients. Music is a hobby of mine, I love it and I play it, so now and then I take a DJ set.
